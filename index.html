The Problem

Groceries in the fridge. No idea what to cook.

Most people don't lack ingredients — they lack the bridge from raw groceries to a finished meal. The result is wasted food, wasted money, and another night of instant noodles or takeout. Existing apps solve fragments of the cooking journey, but none connect inventory to recipe to guidance in one flow.

Tasty

Entertaining recipes, but browsing-first — no personalization to what you already own.

Supercook

Searches by ingredient, but limited AI and poor mobile experience.

Mealime

Strong recipe-to-grocery flow, but starts with recipes — not your fridge.

MyFitnessPal

Gold standard for nutrition, but entirely disconnected from actual cooking.

How It Works

From fridge to table in one flow.

Nourish inverts the typical recipe app. Instead of browsing recipes and building a shopping list, we start with what's already in your kitchen — then handle everything from there.

1

Scan or add your groceries

Snap a photo of your fridge or a grocery receipt and Claude Vision identifies your ingredients. Or type them manually with fuzzy-match autocomplete across 1,000+ items. Your kitchen inventory persists across sessions.

2

Pick your star ingredients

Choose the proteins and carbs you want to feature, or tap "Surprise me" and let Nourish decide. Set your meal type, servings, cuisine, and time budget — preferences saved to your profile.

3

Generate recipes with AI

One tap. Claude Haiku generates 3 structured, skill-appropriate recipes with ingredients, nutrition, and timer metadata — streamed to your screen in seconds.

4

Cook with guided steps

Full-screen cooking mode with large text, step-by-step navigation, integrated countdown timers, and keep-screen-awake — designed for messy hands at arm's length.

5

Rate, save, and keep learning

Rate recipes, save favorites to your cookbook, and build a cooking history. Nourish learns your taste over time — every meal you cook makes the next suggestion better.

Target Users

Who we're building for.

Primary Persona

The Overwhelmed Beginner

Age 22–28 · Students, recent grads, early-career professionals

Has groceries but doesn't know what to make. Intimidated by complex recipes. Defaults to takeout or instant meals. Wants to eat healthier and save money but lacks confidence in the kitchen.

Secondary Persona

The Busy Health-Conscious Cook

Age 28–40 · Working professionals, parents

Knows how to cook but stuck in a rotation of the same 5–7 meals. Wants variety without the planning overhead. Needs quick solutions that fit dietary goals.

"I have chicken, rice, and some random vegetables in my fridge, but I have no idea what to make with them. I usually just order Uber Eats."— Interview participant, matches primary persona

My Contribution

Co-Founder, Product Lead & Everything In Between

Problem definition & research. Conducted 8+ user interviews validating the core hypothesis that people have groceries but lack the bridge to a meal. Synthesized findings into an insights document that shapes every product decision.

Product strategy & pivots. Authored the PRD across 5 major versions and the Development Plan through v5. Led pivots from recipe-to-grocery-list to grocery-to-recipe, added fridge/receipt scanning, redesigned the Cook tab with inline mode switching, and reduced onboarding from 5 steps to 2 — collecting remaining preferences contextually rather than upfront.

Architecture & technical decisions. Selected the stack (React Native/Expo, Node.js/Express, PostgreSQL, Claude API). Key calls: Context API over Redux, Firebase Auth over custom, Claude Vision for scanning, stateless generation with streaming SSE, tool-based structured outputs over prompt-described JSON.

LLM optimization & prompt engineering. Ran a 28-call sensitivity analysis across 7 model/temperature configs to select Haiku 4.5 at 0.95 — the best variety-to-cost ratio. Designed the dual-mode prompt system that handles both ingredient lists and meal names via server-side auto-detection. Built input sanitization (50-char limits, XML tag wrapping, word count filters) to prevent prompt injection. Built A/B testing infrastructure via a Prompt_Versions table for iteration without code deploys.

Design direction. Led Figma wireframing for 7+ core screens and built the Cook tab HTML prototype with animated plate canvas, per-meal-type glow system, star ingredient cards, and the More Options bottom sheet. Prioritized kitchen-friendly UX: large tap targets, arm's-length readability, keep-screen-awake during cooking mode.

Implemented the recipe generation pipeline end-to-end: prompt construction, dual-mode input handling, response validation, safety checks (blocklist scanning, cooking temp verification), prompt caching, and streaming via SSE.
